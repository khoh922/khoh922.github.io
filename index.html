About

I am a Moore Foundation Postdoctoral Fellow in Chemistry at UC Berkeley, working with Prof. Michael Zuerch to study various nonequilibrium quantum phenomena in solids using ultrafast extreme-ultraviolet absorption spectroscopy. I completed my PhD in Physics at MIT under the supervision of Prof. Nuh Gedik, where I studied light-induced structural dynamics in quantum materials using time-resolved X-ray and electron diffraction techniques.

Before joining MIT, I served as a commissioned officer in the Republic of Korea Air Force during my mandatory military service (2017–2020), conducting research at the Agency for Defense Development. During my undergraduate studies at POSTECH, I conducted synchrotron photoemission spectroscopy research in Prof. K. S. Kim’s group (2015–2017). In 2015, I was an exchange student in the Department of Physics at ETH Zurich, Switzerland.

My research focuses on probing the fundamental properties of quantum materials through their ultrafast light-induced dynamics, as well as creating and stabilizing nonequilibrium phases of matter that do not exist under equilibrium conditions through ultrafast optical excitation. I have studied light-induced dynamics in a broad range of quantum systems, including charge-density-wave materials, superconductors, and magnetic materials, using time-resolved scattering and spectroscopy techniques spanning timescales from picoseconds (10⁻¹² s) to attoseconds (10⁻¹⁸ s).
